---

In life we hear many euphamisms, witty sayings, and other colloquial expressions that have just been handed down (some edited) and we just accept them with a little grain of salt.  

To be or not to be.  Is that really the question?  

First off, what does it mean to be?  There is the physical and metaphysical (or spiritual) realms, but is someone who is in a vegetative state really being?  They are their physically, but their mind and personality are not present.  Is that a key characteristic of "to be"?  

But then a tree or a rock are just "being".  A rock can't think or see or move on its own.  But it is still "to be".  Then do we have classifications for different things of "to be"?  Deciding upon characteristics such as movement, growth, or what would be typically thought of as "normal" for that objetc of being.  Is that how we should decide what "to be" means?  

To take a controversial topic, to be a man or to be a woman meant certain physical attributes (and up until recently certain social atmospheres) and characteristics had to be meant.  You couldn't "be" a man or "be" a woman just because you wanted to be.  You had to fit the physical description.  But now there are trans surgeries that can change those physical characteristics.  So then where do we draw the line of man and woma (which should be a hard line)?  Should it be chromosomes? Have boxes saying "birth gender" and "identifying gender"?  Maybe growing a pair of brain cells labeled "common sense" and following the tradition handed down to us over the centuries?  

I don't know, I am not a philosophist or in the gender realm sphere, but I know what I know, and what I know is that God created Man and Woman in His image, each with their own attributes that we know of because humanity has been around to see it for thousands of years.  

Maybe just existing is what it means "to be".  Or there is some characteristics that make it a bit more grey; some that enliven "to be" and some that emphasize "not to be".  Either way, there has to be an agreed upon set of values that set the bar.  Otherwise, there is no point in debating an philosophical question.  

To be alive, or not to be alive.  Is that the "correct" question?
