I am participating in college athletics here at SRJC, and I will be talking about the struggles and accolades that a college athlete goes through in their sport.  

College athletics is hard.  Your practice takes up to 3 hours 5 - 6 days a week, and games are at least 4.5 hours long (for soccer, depending on the sport it may be longer) and if you are traveling, it could be a 12 hour day.  

But the effort is so rewarding.  Going to Kezar Stadium (an iconic place in SF) and playing a scrappy game that you could have lost but won, eating afterwards as a team, having that brotherhood with 35+ guys ... you just don't get that often in life.  There is nothing like the rush of watching your goalkeeper save a penalty kick in a situation that would have swiched the momentum of the game!  

And within that reward comes more attributes that can be applied to every other aspect of life: discipline, time management, communication, hard work ethic, dedication, and learning to sacrifice.  Because you don't just magically get to play a sport at a college.  You have to manage academics on top of playing/practicing and maybe working too.  You have to sacrifice spending time with friends, going to a wedding in order to go to a game (one of my teammates is doing that for our Friday 9/18 game), and you may not be able to work either during the season.  But by doing so, you are achieving a goal, and someone who can sacrifice the right thing for the moment is a man or woman who will be very successful at life.  

It is important to be a good student as much as a good athlete because if you are planning on transferring to a 4 year, one of the first things a coach looks at is your grades!  A 3.0 is minimum to move on, a 3.2 opens up some scholarship opportunities, but a 3.5 or 3.6!  Even the difference between a 3.5 and a 3.6 is thousands of dollars.  You have to have a good GPA to transfer on.  That's why you have to be disciplined and manage your time well.  

Being a student athlete requires sacrifice and buy in.  A buy in of time, effort, and dedication.  You may miss out on things that a regular student would be like, "Why are you missing that?!?  That would be super fun" or "You are missing an important event for a game?"  And that is fine of them to say.  They aren't going to understand the thrill of going to American River College, ending the first half tied 1-1, then absolutely smashing them to win 5-1 and having a great in-N-Out burger meal afterwards and a singing bus ride home. 
Or the disappointment and anger of going to Folsom and having them score on us on a counter attack from our corner, and then the suspense of us having 3 last minute opportunities to score to only lose 0-1.  Someday they might understand why we play the sport we do.  
We do it because we love it, and it is rewarding on so many levels. 

At the end of a day, though, it is just a game.  

But for some it is life.
